Proposed Scope of Works

Below is the full scope of what we'll be designing, supplying, installing and commissioning at 34 High Road. The works follow the extract route and discharge shown on the Kablan Design planning drawings and are grouped into: kitchen extract system, ductwork & distribution, odour control, fresh air supply, gas interlock & controls, charcoal grill assessment, and commissioning & handover.

Kitchen Extract System

  • 4,000mm welded 304 stainless steel canopy — 1.2mm sheet, grease-tight Design, supply and installation of a 4,000 x 1,200mm (nominal) wall canopy in 1.2mm grade 304 stainless steel, fully welded and sealed grease-tight to DW 172 / BS EN 16282-7, with full-length extract plenum, continuous grease channel with removable collection drawers, and sealed IP65 LED luminaires. Serves the two Henny Penny pressure fryers, charcoal grill, Rational combi oven, chip fryer and chip shuttle. Final canopy dimensions confirmed at survey against the appliance line-up and wall length.
  • Stainless steel baffle grease filters with spark-arrestor baffles over the charcoal grill Full set of 500 x 500mm stainless steel baffle grease filters along the canopy, with high-efficiency spark-arrestor baffles over the charcoal grill section to stop embers entering the ductwork. Removable for cleaning.
  • Internally mounted extract fan with inverter speed control Supply and installation of a backward-curved centrifugal cabinet extract fan with IP55 motor out of the airstream, selected for the canopy design extract rate (approximately 2.0 m³/s, confirmed at survey) with the required static margin for the filtration stages, mounted inside the premises as the planning drawings require, on anti-vibration mounts with flexible connections either side. Inverter speed control to set the commissioned duty.
  • In-line attenuator on the fan discharge Rectangular splitter attenuator on the fan discharge, with canvas flexible connections, giving the 10–12 dB(A) reduction indicated on the planning drawings to protect the neighbouring residential amenity.

Ductwork & Distribution

  • Galvanised extract ductwork from canopy to the external riser Lock-formed galvanised mild steel ductwork to DW 144, sized for a duct velocity within the DW 172 range, with all joints sealed, from the canopy plenum through the filtration and fan assembly to the rear wall penetration. Access doors fitted at changes of direction and at the fan for TR19 cleaning.
  • External vertical riser to discharge above eaves External galvanised riser up the rear elevation on galvanised steel brackets with anti-vibration rubber at every fixing, as drawing 48.0726.05, terminating in a vertical high-velocity discharge cowl with bird guard 1.5m above eaves level as shown on drawing 48.0726.03. Riser clad to match the existing wall finish by others (see exclusions).
  • Wall penetration and weathering Core and sleeve the single rear wall penetration for the extract duct, fire-stopped and weathered externally. Structural openings beyond this are by others.

Odour Control

  • Two-stage EU4 pleated pre-filtration Supply and installation of a filter housing with two stages of wire-mesh-supported pleated synthetic EU4 pre-filters with side access, as the planning drawings show, removing fine grease and smoke particulate ahead of the carbon. Replacement interval every 3–4 weeks.
  • Activated carbon filter unit — 20 panel, minimum 0.4 second dwell time Supply and installation of an activated carbon filter housing with 20 no. carbon panels, sized to give a dwell time of not less than 0.4 seconds at the design extract rate. Fried chicken and charcoal grilling put this premises in the very high odour risk band, and with the discharge below ridge height the "very high" level of control applies. Carbon replaced every 8–12 weeks in normal use.

Supply Kitchen Fresh Air System

  • Cabinet supply fan with inverter speed control and G4 filtration Supply and installation of a mechanical fresh air supply fan with G4 inlet filter, selected for approximately 85% of the extract rate so the kitchen runs at a slight negative pressure and cooking odours do not migrate into the shop. Inverter speed control, anti-vibration mounts and flexible connections.
  • Fresh air intake, attenuator and supply ductwork to the canopy External intake louvre with weather protection, in-line attenuator on the supply fan, and galvanised supply ductwork to the canopy front supply plenum so replacement air is delivered at the cooking line rather than across the kitchen.

Gas Interlock & Controls

  • Gas/ventilation interlock panel with gas solenoid valve Supply and installation of a gas interlock control panel with airflow proving on both the extract and supply fans, a normally closed gas solenoid valve on the kitchen gas supply, and a push-to-reset emergency stop knock-off button at the kitchen exit, to BS 6173 and IGEM/UP/19. Gas is shut off automatically if either fan fails or airflow drops below the proven rate.
  • Carbon monoxide detection interlocked to the gas supply and extract fan CO detector in the cooking zone wired into the interlock panel, with an audible/visual alarm and automatic gas shut-off if CO rises above the alarm threshold. Fitted because of the charcoal grill (see the assessment below).
  • Fan control panel, fire alarm and suppression interface Fan control panel with run/fault indication, volt-free interface for the fire alarm and the fire suppression system so the extract runs on and the gas and supply fan shut down on activation. Final power connections from the isolators provided.

Charcoal Grill — Ventilation & Carbon Monoxide Assessment

  • Extract: served from the common canopy, no separate extract required An 800–1,000mm solid-fuel grill under a 4m canopy can be served by the single extract system shown on the planning drawings, provided the canopy is designed for solid-fuel cooking: a higher face velocity over the grill section, spark-arrestor baffles, and no combustible material within the duct route. A separate dedicated grill extract is only needed where the grill cannot sit under the main canopy or the local authority requires it — neither applies here. If the charcoal grill is positioned at the end of the canopy run, we will set the canopy overhang and filter arrangement to suit at survey.
  • Carbon monoxide: CO detection and interlock are required Charcoal produces carbon monoxide continuously, including while smouldering after service. CO detection interlocked to the gas supply and extract fan is included above. The extract fan must run whenever charcoal is lit or smouldering, and the grill must not be used if the extract is off — we will cover this in the handover briefing and the O&M information.
  • Fire safety and cleaning Solid-fuel cooking raises the fire risk in the ductwork: spark-arrestor baffles are included, the canopy and duct will be fully welded/sealed with no plastic components, and TR19 Grease cleaning will be required at three-monthly intervals (heavy use classification) rather than six-monthly. The automatic fire suppression system quoted separately below is specified to cover a solid-fuel appliance.

Commissioning & Handover

  • Full system commissioning and airflow balancing Once installation is complete, we run all systems up, verify supply and extract performance against design, and balance airflows so every area is properly ventilated and pressure-balanced.
  • BESA-compliant installation documentation Installation carried out under our BESA certification (HV020676) to DW 144 / DW 172 standards. Commissioning certificates issued on handover.
  • Product data sheets and client walkthrough on handover Technical data sheets for all installed equipment provided on the day. We walk you through the systems — how to operate them, how to change filters, what to check day to day, and the recommended service schedule.
