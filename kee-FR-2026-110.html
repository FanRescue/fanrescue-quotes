Introduction

Hi Kee,

Here is our proposal for the kitchen extraction, odour control and fresh air works at 61 Rupert Street — a new canopy and very high level odour control system for the open-plan ground floor cooking area, a motorised fresh air supply for the ground floor kitchen, and an upgrade of the existing basement fresh air system.

Proposed Scope of Works

Below is the full scope of what we'll be supplying, installing, and commissioning at 61 Rupert Street. The works are grouped into six areas: kitchen extract system, ductwork and distribution, odour control, supply kitchen fresh air system, the basement fresh air upgrade, and commissioning and handover.

Kitchen Extract System

  • Stainless steel kitchen extract canopy hood — open-plan ground floor front of house Canopy fabricated in 304-grade stainless steel to suit the open-plan cook line, with a dull-polish grain finish appropriate to a front-of-house position in full view of diners. Internal filter housing with removable, washable baffle-type grease filters for high-efficiency grease capture, and integrated canopy lighting.
  • Stainless steel cladding beneath the canopy Stainless steel wall cladding installed beneath the canopy behind the cook line — a hygienic, easy-clean surface that protects the wall from heat and grease and gives a clean finished look to the open kitchen.
  • High-pressure kitchen extract fan High-pressure extract fan selected to overcome the full resistance of the multi-stage odour control train, silencer and front louvre discharge while maintaining the design extract rate at the canopy. Mounted on anti-vibration mountings to limit noise and vibration transfer into the building.

Ductwork & Distribution

  • Extract silencer In-line silencer fitted after the extract fan to reduce fan noise carried through the ductwork to the front discharge and into the surrounding area.
  • Front louvre discharge grille Louvred discharge grille at the front of the building, forming the final discharge point for the fully filtered extract air.
  • Flexible connections throughout Flexible connections at the fan inlet and outlet to isolate fan vibration from the ductwork, canopy and building structure — keeping the system quieter in operation and protecting joints from movement.

Odour Control

  • Very high level odour control system — sized to suit the job Multi-stage odour control unit specified for the very high odour risk of open-plan cooking in a dense mixed-use Soho setting, in line with the BESA EMAQ+ guidance. The filtration train is sized to the extract volume and cooking style so every stage performs as intended.
  • Stage 1 — disposable pre-filter Disposable pre-filter stage capturing residual grease and particulate that passes the canopy baffles, protecting the downstream HEPA and carbon stages and extending their working life. Replaceable on a routine schedule depending on usage.
  • Stage 2 — HEPA filter High-efficiency particulate filtration removing fine smoke and cooking aerosols, so the carbon stage is left to deal with odour alone and the discharge at the front louvre is visibly clean.
  • Stage 3 — activated carbon filter Activated carbon unit sized to give the dwell time needed to treat cooking odours at a very high risk level before discharge. Carbon media is replaceable — we will advise the replacement interval on handover based on how the kitchen runs.

Supply Kitchen Fresh Air System

  • Motorised kitchen fresh air supply — ground floor open-plan cooking Motorised fresh air supply system serving the open-plan ground floor cooking area, replacing the air removed by the extract so the cook line stays comfortable and the canopy captures effectively.
  • Pre-filter to incoming fresh air Pre-filter fitted to the fresh air intake to keep dust and debris out of the supply system and the kitchen.
  • Balanced against the extract Supply volume set at commissioning so the cooking area runs slightly negative to the dining room — helping keep cooking smells and heat at the cook line rather than drifting into front of house.

Basement Fresh Air System — Existing System Upgrade

  • Removal and disposal of the existing fresh air fan Existing basement fresh air fan disconnected, removed and disposed of responsibly off site.
  • New high-pressure fresh air fan with inverter Supply and install a new high-pressure fresh air fan selected to suit the existing system and the extended duct run, with an inverter for variable speed control — allowing the airflow to be set accurately at commissioning and turned down when full duty isn’t needed, reducing running costs and noise.
  • Extension of the existing basement ductwork to the front of the cook line Existing basement fresh air ductwork extended through to the front of the cook line so fresh air is delivered where it is needed, connected into the existing system.

Commissioning & Handover

  • Full system commissioning and airflow balancing Once installation is complete, we run all three systems up, verify extract and supply performance against design, set the basement fan inverter, and balance airflows so the cooking area is properly ventilated and pressure-balanced against front of house.
  • BESA-compliant installation documentation Installation carried out under our BESA certification (HV020676) to DW 144 / DW 172 standards. Commissioning certificates issued on handover.
  • Product data sheets and client walkthrough on handover Technical data sheets for all installed equipment provided on the day. We walk you through the systems — how to operate them, how to change the pre-filters, HEPA and carbon, what to check day to day, and the recommended service schedule.
